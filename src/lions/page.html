    Why this shape

    The roster’s strength is five elite technicians in the middle (Kiefer, Mat, Gabe, Wit, Adrian) and its weakness is center-back pace (Adham 2, Dickey covers). A double pivot of Adrian and Kiefer sits in front of the back four, so the slowest part of the team is never exposed on counters. Mat plays free as the 10, Wit gets isolated one-on-one on the left with George overlapping, and Caleb is a fixed target who does not have to chase. Substitutions slot in without changing the shape.

    Bench by role

    • First subs Maceo Wright (either wing), Jayden Cheung (striker when Caleb rests), William Strimple (midfield or fullback), Anthony Berni (center back).
    • Center-back rotation Micah Lumpkins, Drayman Mcgee, Bennie Sims, Oskar Riendeau.
    • Wide stints Courtney Ayeo, Emmett Dauer, Ryan Jenkins.
    • Keeper Nicholas Farrcor.

    Rotation plan

    1. Caleb comes off at 20–25 minutes for Jayden; Wit stays wide unless Jayden tires, then Wit moves central and Maceo takes the left.
    2. Maceo rotates with Gabe and Wit in 12–15 minute stints; Gabe drops into center midfield whenever Kiefer or Mat rests.
    3. William is the first change for Adrian, Kiefer, George or Kayden Martin.
    4. Berni first for either center back; Micah when the opponent has a fast forward; Drayman, Bennie and Oskar when sitting deep to protect a lead.
    5. Courtney, Emmett and Ryan get 10–12 minute stints on the wings, never both wings at once.
    6. Spread minutes: middle-school leagues usually allow unlimited re-entry, so no starter needs to play 60 straight minutes.

    Formations

    Five shapes this roster can play

    Availability

    The 4-4-2 diamond and 3-5-2 lean hardest on Kicks club players (Adrian, Gabe, Wit, Caleb). The 4-2-3-1 and flat 4-4-2 survive absences best because every slot has a like-for-like substitute.

    Recommended

    4-2-3-1

    Best when
    The default for any opponent.

    Pros

    • Double pivot shields the slow center backs on counters.
    • Mat gets a free role behind the striker.
    • Wit is isolated one-on-one wide with an overlapping fullback.
    • Caleb can be a fixed reference point and does not chase.
    • Every substitution has a like-for-like slot.

    Cons

    • A lone striker with endurance 2 needs a 20–25 minute rotation.
    • Wingers must track back, which Wit and Gabe (endurance 5) can do but Maceo (tackle 2) does less well.
    • When the wingers cut inside, the only width is George and Martin, neither a natural crosser.

    Lineup
    Antonio; George, Adham, Dickey, Martin; Adrian, Kiefer; Wit, Mat, Gabe; Caleb.

    Alternate

    4-4-2 Diamond

    Best when
    Chasing a game, or against a team that packs its own midfield.

    Pros

    • Wit and Caleb as a pair: Caleb pins the center backs, Wit runs in behind them.
    • All four elite midfielders on the field centrally; at this age, owning the middle usually decides the game.
    • Natural passing triangles.

    Cons

    • No natural width; George and Martin must supply all of it.
    • A team with fast wingers drags Adham wide, where he is weakest.
    • Kiefer and Gabe shuttle constantly.
    • Three of its four elite midfielders and both strikers play Kicks club.

    Lineup
    Antonio; George, Adham, Dickey, Martin; Adrian (base), Kiefer (left), Gabe (right), Mat (tip); Wit, Caleb.

    Alternate

    4-3-3

    Best when
    Pressing a weaker team high up the field.

    Pros

    • Natural width and a three-man press.
    • Maceo slots in directly on the right.
    • Fullbacks are freed to stay home.

    Cons

    • A single pivot leaves Adrian alone in front of slow center backs on the counter.
    • Kiefer and Mat must defend as 8s.
    • A high press needs endurance from the striker, and Caleb has 2.
    • Similar to the 4-2-3-1 with more risk and little extra reward.

    Lineup
    Antonio; George, Adham, Dickey, Martin; Adrian; Kiefer, Mat; Wit, Caleb, Gabe (or Maceo).

    Alternate

    3-5-2

    Best when
    Protecting a lead against a big target striker, or a tournament day when center-back legs are the depth you have.

    Pros

    • Uses the roster’s surplus of big center backs (seven of them) and keeps them fresh.
    • George and Martin have the endurance (5) to own a flank.
    • Two strikers.

    Cons

    • Three center backs with speed 3, 2 and 4; one fast winger isolating Adham or Berni is a problem.
    • A beaten wingback has no cover behind him.
    • The most complex shape for 12–13 year olds; the back three must slide as a unit.
    • Both strikers and the base of midfield play Kicks club.

    Lineup
    Antonio; Berni, Adham, Dickey; George (LWB), Martin (RWB); Adrian, Kiefer, Mat; Wit, Caleb.

    Alternate

    4-4-2 Flat

    Best when
    New players are in the lineup, a tough away game, or teaching shape early in the season.

    Pros

    • Every player has one zone to learn.
    • Two banks of four are the hardest thing to break down at this level.
    • Mat drops off Caleb for the second ball.

    Cons

    • Mat is under-used as a second striker.
    • Wide midfielders do two jobs.
    • Center midfield is 2 v 3 against any diamond or 4-3-3 team, so Kiefer and Adrian get outnumbered.

    Lineup
    Antonio; George, Adham, Dickey, Martin; Wit (LM), Adrian, Kiefer, Gabe (RM); Mat (withdrawn), Caleb.
